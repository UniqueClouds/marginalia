> This is a **research-programme sketch**, not a finished argument. It tries to name something larger than the preceding essays: research depends on current model capabilities; startups and organizations reshape themselves at every SOTA release; individuals continually rewrite their workflows to track model upgrades — society has entered a highly **reactive** instability. Three metaphors compete to name it: AI as a massive object exerting gravity; the ground of the AI era as brittle; and an isomorphism between "Attention is all you need" and the structure of social attention. This essay juxtaposes the three, marks their academic neighbors and the open positions, and states honestly: the programme is not yet formed.

## 1. Observations: reactive at three levels

In research, the "re-evaluation tax" is already jargon: every model release forces new eval runs, rewritten prompts, re-baselined comparisons — roughly a day a week at current cadence. In organizations, Moloch-style coordination failure is described again and again: when a new model lands, wrapper firms must integrate and model firms must match, or "look like they're falling behind" — after integration, everyone's net position is unchanged and the engineering time is gone; in summer 2026, five-plus majors launched identical deployment joint-ventures in a single quarter, and the verdict was "that's not conviction, that's a stampede." MIT NANDA's report left the sober footnote: ~95% of generative-AI pilots show no measurable P&L impact. For individuals, psychologists have begun using "change fatigue" for the cognitive toll of continuous adaptation; one author proposes the **Absorption Window** — the time between a capability appearing and the world absorbing it — shrinking toward zero, so that "no plateau is left to stand on"; the coping discourse has slid from model-agnostic to identity-agnostic: even one's professional identity must be held loosely.

## 2. Three metaphors and their neighbors

**Gravity.** The metaphor already has a name: Stefanus.AI's "Intelligence Gravity" describes capital, compute, talent, energy, and nations beginning to orbit "the new centers" — a self-reinforcing field; a16z's capital-flywheel argument uses a blunter image: "imagine a star expanding, swallowing everything around it." Academic neighbors: Rosa's social acceleration and ANT's obligatory passage point — AI is becoming the point that research, startups, and governance must pass through. **Ground.** In engineering this metaphor is mainstream — "most teams discover they are model-dependent the same way you discover a load-bearing wall: by trying to remove it"; "every time a vendor ships, you feel the floor move"; one essay calls the current ecosystem "cathedrals on quicksand." It carries a structural irony: the technology's official name is **foundation models** — and the foundation has a median production lifespan of 12–18 months. **Attention isomorphism.** The most original of the three: large models have become the center of social attention, assigned too much weight — and "whoever controls the weights controls the first draft of reality" is already circulating. Its nearest academic neighbor is an *AI & SOCIETY* paper titled directly after Vaswani's: "Attention is all you need? When responsiveness short-circuits responsibility" — but it takes the ethical route (computational attention vs. Simone Weil's attention as moral act). **The position "social organization isomorphic to the model's mechanism" remains open.**

## 3. Performativity: how a title becomes true

To move isomorphism from metaphor to proposition requires an engine, and MacKenzie's study of finance supplies it: economic theory does not photograph the world like a camera; it is an engine that makes the world more like the theory. Hence the question: **is "Attention Is All You Need" becoming a performative title?** Society reorganizes itself along the paper's self-description — attention as the supreme principle of governance, weights as the vocabulary of allocation — and then cites the reorganized society as evidence that AI is the center of the world. The isomorphism is not discovered; it is performed. Its organizational counterpart is DiMaggio and Powell's mimetic isomorphism: under uncertainty, organizations copy perceived winners — one author counted dozens of "AI-first" press releases after ChatGPT that could swap logos unnoticed.

## 4. A coincidence, as a footnote

Simone Weil wrote two unrelated things: **gravity** (in *Gravity and Grace*, gravity names falling and necessity) and **attention** ("attention is the rarest and purest form of generosity"). The *AI & SOCIETY* paper cites the latter. This essay resists over-interpretation — but it is worth recording that between the programme's two core metaphors, the bridge was built decades ago by the twentieth century's most singular philosopher.

## 5. Operationalization and the surface phenomena

For the programme to hold, the three metaphors must converge into testable propositions. Minimal operationalizations: a time series of the "model-dependence" of research output (share of titles/abstracts naming models); the diffusion of mechanism vocabulary (weights, attention allocation, context window) into management discourse; event studies aligning organizational pivots with release cadence. The relation to the other essays here is mother-concept to surface phenomena: spectacle (018) is the event face of gravity; TokenMaxxing (019) is organizational deformation under pull; AI blackouts (020) are the everyday face of shifting ground; the review crisis (021) is knowledge production bent by the field; and release cycles (024) are the temporal institution of the gravity field itself.

## 6. Open problems (honest list)

The three metaphors must be collapsed into one, or assigned distinct roles — they cannot all be protagonists. Isomorphism, if not operationalized, is only rhetoric. The *AI & SOCIETY* 2025 paper owns the ethical position on attention; this programme must stay on the side of social-organizational isomorphism and performativity. And most fundamentally: this essay currently possesses metaphors, neighbors, and a vacancy — but not yet a proposition that could be refuted. It is written down so that the next draft has something criticizable.
